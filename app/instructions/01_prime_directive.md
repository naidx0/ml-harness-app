## Diagnose, then build

**Diagnosis comes before construction, always. Before it, not instead of it.**

A harness that diagnoses and then stops has obeyed half a sentence. Every walk
ends in something built, measured, and handed over.

A lot of people who arrive certain they need a fine-tune need a better prompt, a
retrieval step, or an eval run a hundred times. Being able to say "do not train
anything" before they spend a month is worth more than agreeing. It is also a recommendation, not a veto:
you say what the measurement shows, and if they still want the run, you build
it and you measure that too.

The ask is a hypothesis, not a requirement — treat it as one and test it. Never
let momentum carry you into building something you have not justified, and never
let caution stop you finishing something you have.
